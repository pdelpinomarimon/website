Unbuilt Infrastructure in the Peruvian-Brazilian Borderlands

Doctoral Research · 2020–2025

Project Overview

My doctoral research examines unbuilt infrastructure in the Peruvian-Brazilian borderlands through the case of the proposed Pucallpa–Cruzeiro do Sul connection.

Proposals to connect Pucallpa, Peru, and Cruzeiro do Sul, Brazil, have circulated in different forms for more than a century. The dissertation examines this long-running infrastructure proposal and its relationship to territorial change, regional integration, and development in western Amazonia.

Three-Paper Dissertation

The dissertation consists of three related papers examining different dimensions of infrastructure development in Amazonia.

Paper 1 · Infrastructure, Territory, Commodities, and Flows

Examines relationships between infrastructure, territory, commodities, and flows in forest regions. It introduces the “tradescapes” framework for examining infrastructure as part of broader networks of investment, production, and trade, and uses the Pucallpa–Cruzeiro do Sul project to illustrate how pressure points within these networks can help identify where future territorial and environmental change may occur.

Published in Current Opinion in Environmental Sustainability (2021)

“'Tradescapes' in the Forest: Framing Infrastructure's Relation to Territory, Commodities, and Flows”

Paper 2 · The Pucallpa–Cruzeiro do Sul Project Over Time

Examines the history of proposals for a connection between Pucallpa and Cruzeiro do Sul and their relationship to changing regional integration agendas.

Manuscript in preparation

Paper 3 · Unbuilt Infrastructure in the Peruvian-Brazilian Borderlands

Examines the Pucallpa–Cruzeiro do Sul project through research in the Peruvian-Brazilian borderlands.

Manuscript in preparation

Research Approach

The dissertation draws on archival research, interviews, and fieldwork conducted in Peru and Brazil, mainly in Ucayali and Acre. The research traces the proposed infrastructure connection across different places, institutions, documents, and periods.
