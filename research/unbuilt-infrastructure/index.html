Unbuilt Infrastructure in the Peruvian-Brazilian Borderlands

Project Overview

My doctoral research examines unbuilt infrastructure in the Peruvian-Brazilian borderlands through the case of the proposed Pucallpa–Cruzeiro do Sul connection.

Proposals to connect Pucallpa, Peru, and Cruzeiro do Sul, Brazil, have circulated in different forms for more than a century. The dissertation examines this long-running infrastructure proposal and its relationship to territorial change, regional integration, and development in western Amazonia.

Three-Paper Dissertation

The dissertation consists of three related papers examining different dimensions of infrastructure development in Amazonia.

Paper 1 — Infrastructure, Territory, Commodities, and Flows

Examines relationships between infrastructure, territory, commodities, and flows in forest regions. It introduces the “tradescapes” framework for examining infrastructure as part of broader networks of investment, production, and trade, and identifies the Pucallpa–Cruzeiro do Sul project as a pressure point within these wider processes.

Published as: “'Tradescapes' in the Forest: Framing Infrastructure's Relation to Territory, Commodities, and Flows” , Current Opinion in Environmental Sustainability (2021).

Read the article →

Paper 2 — The Pucallpa–Cruzeiro do Sul Project Over Time

Examines the longer history of proposals for a connection between Pucallpa and Cruzeiro do Sul and their relationship to changing regional integration agendas.

Manuscript in preparation.

Paper 3 — Unbuilt Infrastructure in the Peruvian-Brazilian Borderlands

Examines the implications of the unimplemented condition of the Pucallpa–Cruzeiro do Sul project in the governance of the Peruvian-Brazilian borderlands.

Manuscript in preparation.

Research Approach

The dissertation draws on archival research, interviews, and fieldwork conducted in Peru and Brazil, mainly in the regions of Ucayali and Acre. The research traces the proposed infrastructure connection across different places, institutions, documents, and periods.
