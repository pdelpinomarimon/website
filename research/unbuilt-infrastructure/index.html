Unbuilt Infrastructure in the Peruvian-Brazilian Borderlands

My doctoral research examines the political and territorial effects of the proposed Pucallpa–Cruzeiro do Sul transportation corridor between Peru and Brazil.

About the research

Although the connection between Pucallpa, Peru, and Cruzeiro do Sul, Brazil, has never been built, the idea of connecting the two cities has influenced regional planning, political debates, conservation strategies, and local expectations for more than a century.

This research examines how infrastructure can produce territorial, political, and environmental effects even when it remains unimplemented.

Research focus

The project explores the relationship between infrastructure, territory, governance, and imagined futures in the Peruvian-Brazilian borderlands.

Support

This research was supported by the Conference of Latin American Geography Field Study Grant, the Clark University Harvard Fund Enhancement Award, the Geller Research Grant, and the SWG Pruitt National Fellowship for Dissertation Research.
