About

I am a geographer and researcher specializing in the relationships between infrastructure, territory, and governance in the Peruvian and Brazilian Amazon.

Drawing on political ecology, spatial analysis, and qualitative fieldwork, I study how infrastructure projects—both built and unbuilt—reshape landscapes, territorial politics, local expectations, and conservation priorities.

I received my Ph.D. in Geography from Clark University in 2025. My dissertation, Unbuilt Infrastructures in the Peruvian-Brazilian Borderlands, examined the political and territorial effects of the proposed Pucallpa–Cruzeiro do Sul transportation corridor.

I currently lead research on the potential social and environmental impacts of the Chancay megaport across the Peruvian Amazon through the Chancay–Amazon Connection Consortium, a project supported by the Gordon and Betty Moore Foundation

I also hold a Master of Regional Planning from Cornell University and degrees in Geography and Environmental Studies from the Pontificia Universidad Católica del Perú.

Research

Infrastructure, Territory and Governance in Amazonia

My research explores how infrastructure shapes territories long before projects are built. I examine the political, environmental, and social consequences of transportation corridors, extractive development, and cross-border integration in Amazonia.

2024–2026

Chancay - Amazon Connection

This collaborative research project examines how the development of the Chancay megaport may reshape connectivity, land use, governance, and conservation across the Peruvian Amazon. Combining political ecology, spatial analysis, and qualitative fieldwork, it explores potential socio-environmental impacts, local expectations, and emerging territorial transformations associated with new Pacific–Amazon infrastructure.

Principal Investigator, Extractives@Clark.
Conducted in partnership with the Center for China and Asia-Pacific Studies (CECHAP) at the Universidad del Pacífico, and Derecho, Ambiente y Recursos Naturales (DAR) . Funded by the Gordon and Betty Moore Foundation.

Project website

2022–2024

Unbuilt Infrastructure in the Peruvian-Brazilian Borderlands

Doctoral research on the anticipated and material effects of the proposed Pucallpa–Cruzeiro do Sul infrastructure corridor, including its influence on territorial expectations, forest politics, and cross-border relations.

Supported by the Conference of Latin American Geography Field Study Grant, the Clark University Harvard Fund Enhancement Award, the Geller Research Grant, and the SWG Pruitt National Fellowship for Dissertation Research.

2021–2022

Mapping for Just Cities

Collaborative research examining the spatial relationships among race, urban change, and contemporary policing in U.S. cities. The project combines geospatial analysis, data visualization, and open-source software development to investigate how patterns of racial stratification, urban investment, and the built environment shape policing practices and spatial inequality.

Research Assistant, Clark University.
Funded by the Urban Studies Foundation.

2020–2021

SESYNC Pursuit: Governing Infrastructure

Collaborative research on infrastructure development, extraction-led land-use change, and forest transformations in the Selva Maya and Western Amazonia.

Research Assistant, Extractives@Clark.
Funded by the National Socio-Environmental Synthesis Center.

2017–2018

Rural Territorial Coalitions and Cross-Border Governance

Master’s thesis research on informal collaboration and territorial governance in the Peru–Bolivia–Brazil tri-border region.

2016–2018

Learning from Flint and Beyond

Research on Michigan’s Emergency Manager Law and its implications for democracy, local governance, and fiscal crisis.

Student Researcher, Cornell University.
